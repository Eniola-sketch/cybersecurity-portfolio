# Medical Records and Electronic Health Records (EHRs)

## What I Learned

I learned that every time a person visits a doctor or healthcare provider, information about their physical and mental health is recorded in an Electronic Health Record (EHR). These records are stored digitally so healthcare professionals can access a patient's medical history when needed.

Medical records can include:
- Personal information
- Medical history
- Diagnoses
- Prescriptions
- Test results
- Mental health information

I also learned that medical data is not only collected in hospitals and clinics. Devices such as fitness trackers and smartwatches can collect health information including:
- Heart rate
- Blood pressure
- Blood sugar levels (supported devices)

This information is often uploaded to cloud services, meaning it is stored online and should also be considered part of a person's medical records.

## Why It Matters in Cybersecurity

Electronic Health Records contain highly sensitive personal information. Because they are stored online, they can become targets for cybercriminals if they are not properly protected.

Healthcare organisations must protect this data by using security measures such as:
- Encryption
- Secure authentication
- Access controls
- Regular system updates

Individuals should also be careful about the health information they share through apps and wearable devices.

## Key Takeaways

- Electronic Health Records (EHRs) store a person's medical information digitally.
- Medical records include both physical and mental health information.
- Fitness trackers and smartwatches also collect medical data.
- Health data stored in the cloud should be treated as sensitive information.
- Protecting medical records is important because they contain private personal information.
